About

Ali Ganjizadeh, MD, MBA

I build and evaluate AI for medical imaging, and I keep asking whether the result is real.

Today I’m a Senior Technical Product Manager at HOPPR, which builds foundation models for medical imaging. I work across the whole life of a model: where the data comes from, how it is de-identified, curated and labeled, what the model is asked to predict, and how it is trained, evaluated and deployed. I joined HOPPR as a research scientist in January 2025 and moved into product that September.

Evaluation is the center of my work. I build the systems that grade a model’s work, including automated graders and LLM-as-judge methods (where one AI model scores another), grounded in how clinicians actually judge a result, and I’ve contributed to published work on measuring medical AI, such as RadEval and RIDGE. I’ve also learned not to trust a good score until I know what the model actually saw: I once traced a model’s quiet loss of accuracy to a preprocessing step that flattened the image intensities a radiologist reads.

Before HOPPR I was a postdoctoral research fellow at the Mayo Clinic Radiology AI & Informatics Lab, where I still collaborate. I built tools that let researchers pull and curate imaging data for themselves, a grant-funded model for detecting prostate cancer recurrence on MRI, and language-model pipelines for extracting data from clinical reports.

I earned my MD at Tehran University of Medical Sciences, then worked as an emergency physician at Susangerd General Hospital in Khuzestan, Iran. Over the same period I was product manager at Neuronio, where we moved an FDA-cleared dementia-screening product from neurology clinics into primary care and grew its revenue sixfold. I also hold an MBA in data science and business analytics.

While in medical school I co-founded ventures in health and medical education, including a healthcare product consultancy that ran due diligence for investors, and a public-health school run as a policy simulation, which received the AMEE Patil Innovation in Teaching Award.

I’m Vice Chair of the AI Education Committee at the Society for Imaging Informatics in Medicine (SIIM), and I help develop AI standards guidelines with Integrating the Healthcare Enterprise (IHE), an initiative RSNA co-founded. I enjoy teaching clinicians and students how to judge an AI tool. Away from a screen, I’m usually on a mountain bike.
